# Novelty audit: sensor-aware Bayesian hypothesis bank with anytime stopping (OTS / fhew)

Scope: candidate novelties 1-4 from README.md section "A sensor-aware Bayesian hypothesis bank with anytime stopping", checked against fhew/likelihood.py, fhew/sensor.py, fhew/dwell.py, fhew/stopping.py, fhew/feasibility.py and docs/methodology_and_results.md (sections 4.5, 4.7, 5.4, 5.5, 10). No repo file was modified. As-of date 2026-11-04. Citations marked "(background, not re-fetched this session)" are well-known works cited from prior knowledge; their bibliographic details should be checked before use.

What is actually implemented (summary for the report writer):
- Sensor model (fhew/sensor.py): slot-synchronous, single emitter; the true channel is detected with probability P_d; every other channel fires independently with probability P_fa. The per-slot emission is reduced to log ratio(s) = log(P_d/P_fa) if s was detected, log((1-P_d)/(1-P_fa)) otherwise; the common all-false-alarm factor cancels across hypotheses. The same object generates data and scores hypotheses ("matched sensor").
- Forward filters (fhew/likelihood.py): (a) DeterministicTrajectoryFilter: exact Bayesian marginalisation over an enumerated set of candidate deterministic trajectories (LFSR states, LCG params, weak seeds, filtered-LFSR states); (b) ChannelHMMFilter: channel-level HMM for Markov / n-gram hypotheses and for H_0 (uniform rows). Baseline NaiveEmission ignores P_fa and penalises misses with eps.
- H_0: a hypothesis in the bank with a uniform (or occupancy / Markov) channel model and a prior floor of 0.15 so it wins no-evidence ties.
- Stopping (fhew/stopping.py): MSPRT = stop when max posterior >= 1-delta; EProcess = E_t(k) = p_k(o_{1:t}) / sum_{j!=k} rho_j p_j(o_{1:t}), stop when >= 1/beta; the docstring and docs (methodology line ~866) state the guarantee only under the mixture-of-competitors null with the truth in the bank.
- Feasibility (fhew/feasibility.py): a rule-based regime classifier (exact recovery if window*hop_rate/dwell*P_d^2 >= 2L; short-horizon prediction if kappa >= 0.25; reactive follower if dwell > reaction latency; else barrage), plus grid builders. Hops are assumed slot-synchronous (no hop-timing estimation inside the bank).

## Q1. Candidate 1 - Has anyone identified a hop-sequence / PN generator through a likelihood with explicit P_d / P_fa (HMM, erasure/insertion channels)?

### Takeaway
Every ingredient is classical (P_d/clutter measurement likelihoods from tracking, HMMs with P_d/P_fa emissions in cognitive-radio spectrum prediction, maximum-likelihood exhaustive state search from correlation attacks, particle filtering of FH signals), but I found no published paper that scores competing hop-sequence *generator families* (LFSR / LCG / weak seed / Markov / unknown) through one shared detection-level P_d/P_fa likelihood. Verdict: NOVEL-IN-COMBINATION / APPLICATION, not a new likelihood or filter.

### Cited Findings
- Detection-level likelihoods with detection probability and clutter (false alarms) are the core of probabilistic data association: the PDAF keeps a target in track despite "clutter and occasional missed detections" by weighting measurements with association probabilities; it originates with Bar-Shalom & Tse (1975, Automatica) and is reviewed in Bar-Shalom, Daum & Huang, "The Probabilistic Data Association Filter," IEEE Control Systems Magazine 29(6):82-100, 2009, DOI 10.1109/MCS.2009.934469 — [NJIT record](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/12313). OTS's ratio P_d/P_fa vs (1-P_d)/(1-P_fa) is the discrete-channel form of the same detection/clutter likelihood ratio (inference; the explicit PDAF formula was not retrieved in this session).
- HMMs whose emission process *is* the sensing error (P_d / P_fa) are established in cognitive-radio spectrum prediction: an IEICE Trans. Commun. paper uses an HMM to forecast next-slot primary-user occupancy and folds the sensor's false-alarm and missed-detection rates into the prediction — [IEICE E93-B p.3274](https://global.ieice.org/en_transactions/communications/10.1587/transcom.E93.B.3274/_p) (title/authors not verified); Wang & Adriman treat the true channel state as a Markov chain observed through noisy sensing (busy reading on idle channel = false alarm) — [EURASIP JWCN 2014](https://jwcn-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s13638-014-0238-5); Liu et al. model sensing as an HMM with P_d and P_f — [optimal sensing interval with imperfect sensing](https://library.tuit.uz/knigiPDF/Ebsco/8-626.pdf).
- FH signals have been tracked with stochastic state-space models and particle filters: Sidiropoulos, Swami & Valyrakis, "Tracking a frequency hopped signal using particle filtering," ICASSP 2006 — [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/icassp/SidiropoulosSV06.html); Valyrakis, Tsakonas, Sidiropoulos & Swami, "Stochastic modeling and particle filtering algorithms for tracking a frequency-hopped signal," IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 57(8), 2009, a nonlinear non-Gaussian model of random carrier frequency and hop timing with closed-form optimal importance function — [UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/stochastic-modeling-and-particle-filtering-algorithms-for-trackin/); multichannel extension at CAMSAP 2007 — [UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/multichannel-particle-filters-for-tracking-a-frequency-hopped-sig/). These track the frequency at IQ level; they do not identify a generator family.
- Random-finite-set (PHD) filtering of FH spectrum tracks, which natively includes detection probability and clutter, has been applied to time-sparse FH signals with a periodicity-alignment likelihood ratio: Wang et al., "Dynamic Spectrum Tracking of Multiple Targets With Time-Sparse Frequency-Hopping Signals," IEEE Signal Process. Lett., 2024 — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/a43e9f5afc7c569da9705f2c890a79f2/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Missing/gap-ridden FH observations are explicitly modelled in blind FH parameter estimation: HMM-enhanced Bayesian compressive sensing for hop-time estimation with up to 30% missing observations (Wang et al., IEEE Commun. Lett., 2022) — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/799d4e07f33451abb442725ea228a9d3/?utm_source=claude_desktop); structure-aware Bayesian compressive sensing for FH spectrum estimation with missing observations (Liu et al., IEEE Trans. Signal Process., 2016) — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f0c20fb086ac5eeeb74f0914a96ccc75/?utm_source=claude_desktop). A 2023 CAC paper proposes an FH-pattern metric "NRMSE including missed detection" to score missed and false detections — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9e7b9a3be7275106952a968abe21094a/?utm_source=claude_desktop). These are parameter/pattern estimators, not generator identifiers.
- LFSR reconstruction from corrupted output is a mature cryptanalytic line: Golić & Mihaljević's generalized correlation attack uses (constrained) Levenshtein distance, i.e. insertions/deletions, for irregularly clocked generators (J. Cryptology 3:201-212, 1991), later refined into a statistically optimal probabilistic edit-distance procedure (Golić & Petrović), and Mihaljević & Golić studied iterative LFSR initial-state reconstruction from a noisy output sequence — [search summary of Springer/IACR sources](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-49264-X_20.pdf), [Golić CRYPTO 2001 (shrinking generator fast correlation attack)](https://www.iacr.org/archive/crypto2001/21390439.pdf).
- Blind scrambler/LFSR reconstruction from noisy bitstreams: Cluzeau, ISIT 2004 and "Reconstruction of a Linear Scrambler," IEEE Trans. Computers 2007; Xie, Wang & Huang, JSEE 25(4):560, 2014 (eigen-analysis + Walsh-Hadamard); He, IET Commun. 2015 frames feedback-polynomial identification as hypothesis testing; Liu et al., IEEE TIFS 2013 handle noisy channels after a channel encoder — [JSEE 2014](https://www.jseepub.com/EN/Y2014/V25/I4/560) (other items from search metadata only).
- The author's own review already cites Kim et al. (IEEE TAES 2025) on non-cooperative spreading-code estimation via the LFSR linear relation and Berlekamp-Massey — see E:\Desktop\ots\research_notes\OTS novelty claims audit\_attached_lit_review.txt ref [24] (not independently verified here).
- An informal 2026 public GitHub project recovers a 20-bit Fibonacci LFSR seed and polynomial "from sparse, observed frequency-hop channel detections" by exhaustive search over 2^20 seeds scored by a plain match count (no P_d/P_fa likelihood, no multiple families, no unknown class, no stopping rule) — [yashagarwal-lab/lfsr-frequency-hop-sequence-recovery](https://github.com/yashagarwal-lab/lfsr-frequency-hop-sequence-recovery). This is not peer-reviewed, but it shows that "LFSR hop recovery from sparse detections by state search" is publicly in circulation.
- US patents on drone detection model "linear and LFSR-based hopping sequences" with a few parameters and note that each detection may be a true transmission or a false alarm — [US 11552674](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11552674), [US 11894870](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11894870), [US 11190233](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11190233). Full text could not be read (scanned-image PDF); claims and fitting method are unverified.
- Siegenthaler's correlation attack (IEEE Trans. Computers, 1985) is an exhaustive search over LFSR initial states scored by agreement with a noisy keystream, i.e. maximum-likelihood state search under a binary symmetric channel (background, not re-fetched this session).

### Inferences
- OTS's DeterministicTrajectoryFilter is mathematically a Siegenthaler-style ML/Bayesian exhaustive state search, with the BSC replaced by a detection channel (erasure + substitution + extra detections at known slot positions). Calling it a new "gap-robust Berlekamp-Massey" over-claims; "state-enumeration (correlation-attack-style) likelihood under a detection-level channel" is accurate.
- OTS assumes slot-synchronous observation: misses appear at *known* slot positions, so the channel has erasures/substitutions/extra symbols but no deletions or insertions in the Levenshtein sense. The Golić-Mihaljević edit-distance literature handles a harder synchronization problem; OTS should not claim robustness to insertions/deletions in that sense, and should note it does not estimate hop timing (the blind-FH literature above does).
- The "matched sensor" design (same P_d/P_fa object generates and scores) means reported robustness is under a correctly specified sensor. Robustness to mis-specified P_d/P_fa is a separate claim that needs its own experiment.
- The NaiveEmission ablation is a fair internal comparison but a weak external baseline; a reviewer will expect comparison with a correctly specified PDA/erasure-aware correlation attack, which is what the sensor-aware filter already is.
- Defensible wording: "To our knowledge, prior FH-prediction and LFSR-reconstruction work either assumes clean consecutive outputs, models noise as a symmetric substitution channel, or treats misses only as missing samples; we score every generator hypothesis (algebraic, seed-search, Markov, and none-of-the-above) through a single detection-level likelihood with explicit P_d and P_fa, using the standard HMM forward recursion. The likelihood itself is the standard detection/clutter model from target tracking and cognitive-radio sensing; the contribution is applying it uniformly across a generator-identification bank."

## Q2. Candidate 3 - Has MSPRT or e-process / anytime-valid stopping been applied to emitter, generator, or protocol identification?

### Takeaway
The MSPRT as implemented (stop when the top posterior >= 1-delta) is exactly Baum & Veeravalli's 1994 MSPRT, already motivated by spread-spectrum acquisition and used for target classification and modulation recognition; the e-process is a likelihood ratio against a Bayes mixture, which is a classical SPRT-type test martingale with Ville's 1939 bound. I found no RF/emitter paper using the e-value / e-process vocabulary, so the application is new to my knowledge, but the statistics are NOT NOVEL. Verdict: NOT NOVEL as method; NOVEL-IN-APPLICATION (weak) for e-process stopping in FH generator identification.

### Cited Findings
- Baum & Veeravalli, "A sequential procedure for multihypothesis testing," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, 1994, define the MSPRT, motivate it with "direct-sequence spread spectrum signal acquisition, multiple-resolution-element radar," derive error-probability bounds and show it needs two to three times fewer samples than Bayesian fixed-sample tests — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6ed7854bb0b65f7eac9c4459158cb464/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Dragalin, Tartakovsky & Veeravalli, "Multihypothesis sequential probability ratio tests - Part I: Asymptotic optimality," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, 1999, prove asymptotic optimality of both the Bayesian MSPRT and a GLR version, including for correlated, non-homogeneous observation processes (which covers HMM likelihoods) — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ce76bd6b242f5a58a3ce30b10875c971/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- MSPRT-type tests are already used for classification in sensing: Gao et al., "Tracking-Aided Classification of Targets Using Multihypothesis Sequential Probability Ratio Test," IEEE Trans. Aerosp. Electron. Syst., 2018 (Armitage sequential test with embedded tracking) — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/46a7defb234852498c3b82b4131aaec4/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- SPRT for modulation recognition predates OTS by ~30 years: Lin & Kuo (?) "Modulation recognition via sequential probability ratio test," 1995 — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ab418ea7d7f55b4fa0f6945d7a0be38e/?utm_source=claude_desktop); "Sequential modulation classification of dependent samples," ICASSP 1996, with Markov-chain signal models and a "controllable classification error rate" — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6f060e5ab2575ae492ef066ef06f80c8/?utm_source=claude_desktop); fixed-sample LRT modulation classification as multiple-hypothesis testing (Panagiotou et al., MILCOM 2000) — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/edc6c648bcf752e7b66b5eb521b71fa5/?utm_source=claude_desktop). (Second author of the 1995 paper not verified.)
- E-processes are nonnegative supermartingales under the null; Ville's inequality bounds the probability of ever crossing 1/alpha by alpha, which is what makes stopping valid at any time — survey: Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk & Shafer, "Game-theoretic statistics and safe anytime-valid inference," Statistical Science 38(4):576-601, 2023, DOI 10.1214/23-STS894 — [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-38/issue-4/Game-Theoretic-Statistics-and-Safe-Anytime-Valid-Inference/10.1214/23-STS894.full).
- A 2026 preprint notes that the e-value test martingale built from likelihood ratios "matches the SPRT if no lower bound to accept H_0 is specified," i.e. Wald's SPRT is a special case of an e-process test — [arXiv 2606.00878](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00878).
- Searches for e-values / e-processes / anytime-valid inference applied to spectrum sensing, radar, modulation classification or emitter ID returned only general theory, with no applied wireless paper — [search result set incl. arXiv 2501.03982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03982).
- Composite-null anytime validity via likelihood ratios against a maximum or a split estimate is standard ("universal inference": Wasserman, Ramdas & Balakrishnan, PNAS 117(29), 2020; "safe testing": Grünwald, de Heide & Koolen, JRSSB 2024) (background, not re-fetched this session).

### Inferences
- Statistical status of OTS's EProcess: E_t(k) = p_k / sum_{j!=k} rho_j p_j is a Bayes factor against a prior-weighted mixture of competitors. It is a test martingale only under that mixture (the code and docs say so honestly). Under a *specific* competitor j it is bounded by p_k/(rho_j p_j), so Ville gives only P(false-ID of k | H_j) <= beta/rho_j, which is much weaker than beta. Claim wording such as "false-identification bound valid at any stopping time" without the "under the prior-weighted mixture of in-bank competitors" qualifier would over-claim.
- A cheap fix with a stronger, per-competitor guarantee: replace the mixture denominator by max_{j!=k} p_j(o_{1:t}). Under any true in-bank H_j, p_k/max_{i!=k} p_i <= p_k/p_j, which is a test martingale under p_j, so P(ever declaring k | H_j) <= beta for every j. This is the Armitage / matrix-SPRT and GLR-MSPRT statistic of Dragalin et al. (1999), so it is also classical. Caveat: each family likelihood p_j is itself a uniform mixture over enumerated seeds/states, so "under H_j" means "seed drawn from the bank's prior over seeds"; for a fixed adversarial seed the bound inflates by the inverse prior weight of that seed.
- When the truth is H_0-like (keyed CSPRNG), the uniform H_0 likelihood is, up to computational indistinguishability, the true law, so the Ville bound on wrongly declaring a deterministic family applies approximately. That is a nice defensible statement. For a structured out-of-bank generator (e.g. filtered LFSR outside the bank), no likelihood in the bank is the truth and no anytime-valid guarantee holds; the docs already acknowledge this (methodology line ~866).
- If H_0 is the "occupancy" or "markov" plug-in variant, its sequential (prequential) predictive likelihood is still a valid likelihood for a test martingale only if fitted strictly on past data; this should be stated.
- MSPRT with posterior threshold 1-delta is Baum-Veeravalli verbatim; its error guarantee is Bayesian (average over the prior), not frequentist per-hypothesis. The observed E[tau] proportional to log(1/delta) and 1/P_d (exp11) reproduces textbook MSPRT asymptotics (Dragalin et al.), consistent with KL per detected hop; it is a sanity check, not a finding.
- Defensible wording: "We use the classical MSPRT (Baum & Veeravalli 1994) and, alternatively, a likelihood-ratio e-process (Ramdas et al. 2023) over the generator bank; to our knowledge this is the first use of anytime-valid (e-process) stopping for FH generator identification. The false-identification bound holds under the prior-weighted mixture of in-bank competitors [or: per in-bank competitor, if the max-denominator variant is adopted]; we validate empirically against nominal beta."

- Did not verify whether conformal e-detectors or e-process change detectors have been applied to RF jamming detection specifically (the author's review mentions them generically).

## Q3. Candidate 2 - Is a mandatory none-of-the-above class H_0 (open-set rejection) new for emitter / generator identification, and how does OTS's Bayesian H_0 differ from RF open-set methods?

### Takeaway
A "no-match" / reject decision in multi-hypothesis classification is classical (Gutman 1989 proves a likelihood-ratio test with a no-match decision is asymptotically optimal for Markov sources), and open-set rejection is an active topic in RF fingerprinting and SEI. OTS differs in mechanism - H_0 is a generative likelihood (uniform/occupancy/Markov) competing inside the Bayes posterior, not a threshold on a discriminative score - but generative "background model" likelihood ratios are also standard. Verdict: NOT NOVEL as a concept; NOVEL-IN-APPLICATION for FH generator identification (and the "uniform = CSPRNG" interpretation is well motivated).

### Cited Findings
- Gutman, "Asymptotically optimal classification for multiple tests with empirically observed statistics," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, 1989: for M hypotheses with Kth-order Markov sources, "a likelihood ratio test that is allowed to produce a no-match decision is shown to provide asymptotically optimal error probabilities and minimum no-match decisions" — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f408449295755e79bf43e768163f3a98/?utm_source=claude_desktop).
- Open-set settings in RF fingerprinting are where "previously unseen, unauthorized, or adversarial transmitters can appear at test time"; most schemes tune rejection thresholds manually or per class — [open-set RF fingerprinting topic summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/open-set-rf-fingerprinting) (aggregator; treat as secondary).
- Representative RF open-set methods: generative outlier augmentation to reject unseen transmitters (Karunaratne et al., 2021) — [arXiv 2108.13099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13099v1); open-set wireless transmitter authorization with deep learning and dataset considerations — [arXiv 2005.00954](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2005.00954); OpenMax replacing softmax for known/unknown emitter rejection (CNN-FAN, 2026) — [J. Data Acquisition & Processing](https://sjcj.nuaa.edu.cn/sjcjyclen/article/abstract/202604009); open-world RF fingerprinting with unlabeled known+unknown emitters at AAAI — [AAAI proceedings](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/32003/34158).
- Chow's reject option (1970) and the GMM-UBM likelihood ratio against a universal background model in speaker verification (Reynolds et al., 2000) are the standard generative precedents for scoring each class against a catch-all background likelihood (background, not re-fetched this session).
- Statistical randomness testing / distinguishing attacks use "the source is uniform random" as the null against which a generator's structure must be demonstrated (e.g. NIST SP 800-22) (background, not re-fetched this session).

### Inferences
- What is genuinely different from RF open-set work: OTS's H_0 is a *calibrated generative hypothesis with a defined likelihood*, so it participates in the posterior and in the e-process/MSPRT error accounting; discriminative open-set methods (OpenMax, outlier exposure) give a score without such a probabilistic error statement. That is a defensible point of contrast, but it is the old generative-vs-discriminative open-set distinction, not a new idea.
- H_0 as "uniform channel law" has a crisp meaning for a keyed CSPRNG (computationally indistinguishable from uniform), which justifies "routes to unknown" on CSPRNG. It does not imply rejection of a *structured* out-of-bank generator: the docs show filtered LFSR falls back to the Markov expert, and the stale-bank test routes to unknown only 30-87% of the time (docs 5.4-5.5). Claims should say "avoids hard false identification of a deterministic family" (which the data support: 0.00-0.03 hard false-ID) rather than "detects unknown generators."
- The prior floor pi_0(H_0) = 0.15 is a design heuristic; reviewers will ask for sensitivity to it.
- The "pairwise SPRT with no H_0 false-identifies 100%" comparison is a straw man in the sense that any closed-set classifier must name a class; the fairer baseline is a closed-set classifier with a threshold/reject option (Chow) or Gutman's no-match test.
- Defensible wording: "Following the classical no-match / reject-option idea (Gutman 1989; Chow 1970), the bank always contains an explicit generative none-of-the-above hypothesis H_0 whose uniform law models a keyed generator; unlike score-threshold open-set RF classifiers, H_0 enters the posterior and the stopping-rule error accounting directly."

### Gaps
- Did not find a paper that specifically adds a uniform-random catch-all hypothesis to a bank of PRNG/LFSR/hop-generator hypotheses; absence of evidence in a ~20-query search is not proof.
- Could not verify the authorship lists of arXiv 2005.00954 and the AAAI open-world RF paper from fetched pages.

## Q4. Has anyone identified the PRNG family of an FH radio (or any generator) from intercepts?

### Takeaway
PRNG-family identification from *clean* output sequences exists (ML classifiers, decision trees on LCG/LFSR outputs), and hop-sequence inference with a *known* generator family exists for Bluetooth (BlueEar) and in patents/informal code for LFSR-based drone hopping; I found no peer-reviewed work that identifies an FH emitter's generator *family* from lossy passive detections with a probabilistic sensor model and an unknown class. This supports a carefully scoped "to our knowledge" claim for the integrated problem formulation.

### Cited Findings
- A 2024 hybrid RNN-CNN model identifies which PRNG produced a sequence with >95% reported accuracy, trained on 4,000 sequences per generator — [CEUR-WS Vol-3829 short6](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3829/short6.pdf); [KUBG repository](https://elibrary.kubg.edu.ua/id/eprint/50187/).
- An older IEEE paper used the C4.5 decision-tree learner on LCG and LFSR outputs and reached 100% accuracy for LFSRs — [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/29428806.pdf) (authors/venue not verified).
- A 2021 visualization study classifies PRNGs via Markovian transition matrices of their outputs — [Machicao et al. 2021 PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/visvar/visvar.github.io/main/pdf/machicao2021a.pdf).
- BlueEar (Albazrqaoe, Huang & Xing, MobiSys 2016) uses two commodity radios to learn the hopping sequence of undiscoverable Bluetooth and "integrates statistical models and a lightweight machine learning algorithm to predict the adaptive hopping behavior," keeping >90% packet capture — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.cityu.edu.hk/~jhuan9/papers/blueear16mobisys.pdf); a later Energies 2021 paper notes BlueEar gives no explicit AFH-map prediction accuracy — [Korea Univ. record](https://scholar.korea.ac.kr/handle/2021.sw.korea/129568?mode=full).
- Non-cooperative FH reconnaissance papers assume the FH sequence "is usually generated by a certain model with certain regularity" and predict it with LSTMs, not by generator identification (Li et al., China Communications 2021) — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8d2c8a037d7e5b3dbf44d13e0d0185c7/?utm_source=claude_desktop).

### Inferences
- The ML PRNG-identification papers are closed-set and assume clean outputs; OTS's distinctive features relative to them are the sensor model, the explicit H_0, and sequential stopping, which is consistent with verdicts in Q1-Q3.
- BlueEar is the most practically relevant precedent (real hop inference from a lossy sniffer); OTS should cite it and distinguish "known protocol generator with unknown clock/AFH map" from "unknown generator family."

### Gaps
- Military TRANSEC / SINCGARS / HAVE QUICK hop-generator analysis is unlikely to be in open literature; none was found.
- Spill & Bittau, "BlueSniff: Eve meets Alice and Bluetooth" (USENIX WOOT 2007) is a further Bluetooth hop-recovery precedent (background, not re-fetched this session).

## Q5. Candidate 4 - Are the feasibility / predictability bounds (fhew/feasibility.py) novel?

### Takeaway
They are not bounds in a theorem sense but a rule-based regime classifier built from classical facts (Berlekamp-Massey needs ~2L clean consecutive outputs; follower jamming works when dwell exceeds reaction latency; normalized-entropy predictability). Verdict: NOT NOVEL as theory; at most a useful operational presentation (NOVEL-IN-APPLICATION, weak). One rule also appears mis-specified.

### Cited Findings
- Berlekamp-Massey finds the shortest LFSR for a fully known sequence; linear complexity L is the length of that shortest LFSR — [Encyclopedia of Mathematics, linear complexity](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Linear_complexity_of_a_sequence); the 2L-sample requirement is from Massey (IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 15(1), 1969), already cited by the author ([lit review ref 19](E:\Desktop\ots\research_notes\OTS novelty claims audit\_attached_lit_review.txt)).
- Erasures at known positions are handled in algebraic decoding via the Berlekamp-Massey / error-locator connection; LFSR synthesis with errors is studied as k-error linear complexity — [search summary pointing to Wikipedia BM and k-error linear complexity theses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlekamp%E2%80%93Massey_algorithm).
- The follower-jammer trade-off (sensing advantage vs hop rate) recurs throughout the anti-jamming literature cited by the author (Hanawal et al., IEEE TMC 2016; Wang & Wang, Trans. Emerg. Telecommun. Technol. 2022) — [lit review refs 9, 18](E:\Desktop\ots\research_notes\OTS novelty claims audit\_attached_lit_review.txt); textbook treatment in Torrieri, *Principles of Spread-Spectrum Communication Systems* (background, not re-fetched this session).
- Entropy-based limits of predictability (Fano-inequality bound on maximum predictability from sequence entropy) are established in Song, Qu, Blumm & Barabási, "Limits of predictability in human mobility," Science 327:1018-1021, 2010, DOI 10.1126/science.1177170 (background, not re-fetched this session).

### Inferences
- Code-level concern (fhew/feasibility.py, can_recover_exactly): clean_consecutive = distinct_hops * P_d**2 treats "a gap breaks the run" with an exponent of 2, but the probability that a specific run of 2L consecutive hops is fully detected is P_d^(2L), and the expected waiting time for such a run grows roughly like P_d^(-2L). At L=7, P_d=0.9 the rule says 0.81 x hops >= 14, whereas P_d^14 is about 0.23. The rule therefore overstates exact-recovery feasibility for plain BM (though the particle-filter "state-filtered" recovery does not need consecutive runs, so the right boundary for OTS's own method is a likelihood/information criterion, not a BM run-length). It should not be presented as a bound.
- kappa = 1 - H(p_hat)/log M is a normalized-entropy score of the *model's* predictive, not a bound on achievable predictability (Song et al.'s Fano-type quantity bounds the latter); naming it a "predictability bound" would over-claim.
- The "predictability boundary" narrative (keyed + short dwell defeats both prediction and follower jamming) is correct and useful but follows from classical facts; defensible wording: "an operational feasibility map that composes classical conditions (BM's 2L clean outputs, follower-jamming dwell > latency, entropy-based predictability) into a single regime classifier."

### Gaps
- Did not find a published "feasibility map" for EW strategy selection over (window, hop-rate, dwell, latency); such maps may exist in defence textbooks (e.g. Poisel, Adamy) that were not searched.

## Q6. Overall verdicts and defensible claim wording

### Takeaway
None of the four components is novel in isolation; the defensible contribution is the integration - a single detection-level P_d/P_fa likelihood shared by a heterogeneous generator bank (algebraic, seed-search, Markov, uniform none-of-the-above) with classical MSPRT and likelihood-ratio e-process stopping, applied to passive FH generator identification - plus its empirical characterization. Claims should be phrased "to our knowledge" and anchored to the classical sources above.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Verdict table:

- Recommended headline claim: "To our knowledge, this is the first passive FH generator-identification system in which every candidate generator family - including an explicit none-of-the-above hypothesis for keyed generators - is scored through a common detection-level likelihood with explicit P_d and P_fa, and identification is stopped by a classical MSPRT or a likelihood-ratio e-process with a stated false-identification bound."
