# Novelty audit: cryptanalytic / sequence-recovery experts (LCG, adaptive BM, seed beam, rule search, lossy-intercept recovery)

Scope: `fhew/experts.py` (`LCGCryptanalysisHypothesis`, `AdaptiveBMHypothesis`, `SeedBeamHypothesis`, `RuleSearchHypothesis` + `default_rule_library`), `fhew/recovery.py` (GF(2) Berlekamp–Massey), `fhew/generators.py` (`LCGGenerator`, `WeakSeedGenerator`), `docs/CHANGELOG.md` entry of 2026-11-03. Audit date 2026-11-04. Repo files were read, not changed.

**What the code does.** These facts come from reading the source, and they drive the verdicts:
- `LCGCryptanalysisHypothesis` attacks only `lcg_full`, an LCG whose **modulus is known and equal to M** and whose **channel is the full state** (no truncation). It takes a clean consecutive run of at least 6 outputs and computes `a = (x2-x1)(x1-x0)^-1 mod M` and `c = x1 - a*x0`. It then checks this against the run, sliding the start forward to drop leading outputs that do not fit. The run must be gap-free. After it locks, it rolls forward across gaps and re-anchors only on a clean hop that agrees with the prediction, which rejects ghost hops. The docstring says the high-bit-truncated LCG is handled by the seed-search experts instead.
- `SeedBeamHypothesis(family="lcg")` attacks `LCGGenerator` with **known** modulus 2^16 and **known** glibc-style constants (a = 1103515245 mod 2^16, c = 12345). Only the seed is unknown, and the channel is `(x >> shift) mod M`. It brute-forces all 65,536 seeds as a vectorised Bayesian weight array, scores each through the P_d/P_fa sensor emission, and prunes candidates more than 25 nats behind the best. `family="weak_seed"` does the same over 2^18 seeds of a splitmix64-keyed PRNG.
- `AdaptiveBMHypothesis` runs standard GF(2) Berlekamp–Massey on the bit-expansion of a **contiguous clean run**. Any slot without a clean detection resets the run. The window is about (8/3)L/b hops, the register length is at most 32, and M must be a power of two. A fit is dropped after two contradicting clean hops, then BM is re-run.
- `RuleSearchHypothesis` runs Bayesian model averaging over a finite, enumerated library of deterministic recurrences: about 140k rules at M = 16 (heavy), or about 10k (light). The prior is uniform over families, then uniform within each family. Each rule fills gaps with its own predictions. Rules are scored by the sensor-aware likelihood and pruned at 18 nats behind the best. The library restarts after 2 surprises once locked, carrying the old survivors at half mass. Ghost-aware re-sync means a rule re-syncs only after two disagreeing clean hops when false alarms are possible.

## Q1. LCG parameter recovery from channel indices (`LCGCryptanalysisHypothesis`; LCG arm of `SeedBeamHypothesis`)

### Takeaway
**NOT NOVEL.** Recovering (a, c) of a full-output LCG with known modulus from three or four consecutive outputs is textbook. It predates Reeds (Cryptologia 1977), which already recovers the modulus as well. Truncated LCGs with secret parameters were broken by Boyar/Plumstead (1982/1989), Stern (1987) and Frieze et al. (1988), and lattice refinements continue to 2020–2024. Brute force over a 2^16 seed space with known constants is a trivial exhaustive search. A defensible claim is engineering only: these known attacks were wrapped as online Bayesian experts that tolerate missed and phantom hops.

### Cited Findings
- Reeds, "'Cracking' a Random Number Generator", *Cryptologia* 1(1):20–26, Jan 1977. It argues LCGs are unsuitable for cryptography and recovers modulus, multiplier and increment from a few observed outputs — [Reeds PDF (UW-Madison course mirror)](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~cs812-1/Reeds.pdf); [UMD notes](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/COURSES/456/F19/notes/crackrand.pdf)
- Plumstead, "Inferring a sequence generated by a linear congruence", FOCS 1982, pp. 153–159 (DOI 10.1109/sfcs.1982.73). It predicts an LCG from a few outputs with multiplier, increment and modulus all unknown. The 1983 *Advances in Cryptology* version says Reeds' similar result assumed factoring is easy — [Springer chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/inferring-a-sequence-generated-by-a-linear-congruence/13869108); [Scilit](https://www.scilit.net/publications/1ee35bbec0e9fd4475892dfead3752e0)
- Boyar gave an efficient algorithm for predicting LCGs when O(log log m) low bits are discarded and the parameters are unknown. Stern gave a polynomial-time lattice algorithm for truncated LCGs with all parameters unknown. Frieze et al. gave a polynomial-time lattice algorithm when m and b are known, which may fail for a small set of exceptional multipliers. These summaries come from the related-work section of Yu et al., ePrint 2022/1134 — [IACR ePrint 2022/1134](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1134.pdf)
- Contini & Shparlinski, "On Stern's attack against secret truncated linear congruential generators", ACISP 2005, LNCS 3574, pp. 52–60 (DOI 10.1007/11506157_5). They give a simpler, more robust Stern-style attack that needs less data, and characterise its limits — [Macquarie research portal](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/on-sterns-attack-against-secret-truncated-linear-congruential-gen/)
- Recent extensions: Sun, Zhu & Zheng (Des. Codes Cryptogr. 2020) extend Stern's algorithm to truncated multiple recursive generators (order-k recurrences) with unknown parameters. Yu et al. (ePrint 2022/1134) improve the truncated-LCG/MRG inference. Wang & Dai (ePrint 2024/1604) recover parameters and initial state of multiple matrix congruential generators from truncated outputs by lattice reduction — [Springer DCC 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-020-00729-8); [ePrint 2022/1134](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1134.pdf); [ePrint 2024/1604](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1604)
- A pedagogical reference (Pommerening, Univ. Mainz) on predicting LCGs, including the algebraic difference/inverse method used by `recover_lcg_full` — [Mainz LCGcrack](https://www.staff.uni-mainz.de/pommeren/Kryptologie/Bitstrom/2_Analyse/LCGcrack.html)
- Further citations, not re-fetched this session (bibliographic data from memory; confirm before citing):
  - J. Boyar, "Inferring sequences produced by a linear congruential generator missing low-order bits", *J. Cryptology* 1(3), 1989 (DOI believed to be 10.1007/BF02252875; a PDF with that DOI appeared in search results but was not opened).
  - J. Stern, "Secret linear congruential generators are not cryptographically secure", FOCS 1987.
  - A. Frieze, J. Håstad, R. Kannan, J. Lagarias, A. Shamir, "Reconstructing truncated integer variables satisfying linear congruences", *SIAM J. Comput.* 17(2), 1988.

### Inferences
- `recover_lcg_full` solves the easiest case in this literature: known modulus, untruncated output, consecutive clean outputs. It is weaker than Reeds (1977) and Plumstead (1982), which also recover the modulus. A novelty claim for the algebra is not defensible.
- Dropping leading outputs that do not fit is a sliding-window consistency check. That is a robustness detail, not a cryptanalytic contribution.
- Truncated outputs and erasures. The lattice literature (Frieze et al.; Stern; Contini–Shparlinski) models outputs as known high bits at **known time indices**. Writing x_{i+k} = a^k x_i + c(a^k−1)/(a−1) mod m lets you use non-consecutive known indices. I infer, without having checked each paper's model, that erasures at known slots fit these formulations. Phantom hops (insertions) do not; the lattice papers assume error-free truncated outputs.
- The OTS truncated-LCG attack (`SeedBeamHypothesis`, `lcg`) uses no lattice. It enumerates 2^16 seeds with known (a, c, m), which is computationally trivial. It handles missed and phantom hops through a likelihood with pruning. That tolerance is real but comes from the generic Bayesian filter, not from any LCG-specific insight.
- Defensible wording: "We wrap the classical algebraic LCG attack (Reeds 1977; Plumstead/Boyar 1982/1989) as an online expert that locks from clean consecutive hops and then rolls forward across sensor gaps, and handles small-modulus truncated LCGs by exhaustive seed search under a P_d/P_fa sensor likelihood." Avoid "novel LCG cryptanalysis" and "recovers LCG parameters" without saying that the modulus is known and the output is untruncated.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper on LCG inference from outputs with **insertions** (false detections at unknown positions) or **unknown-position deletions**. This combination might be under-studied in the cryptographic literature, but my search was not exhaustive (2 queries), so this absence is weak evidence.
- Boyar 1989, Stern 1987 and Frieze et al. 1988 were not opened. Their exact output models (consecutive or arbitrary known indices) are unverified.

## Q2. Berlekamp–Massey with an adaptive window, LFSRs up to length 32, dropping a fit after two contradictions (`AdaptiveBMHypothesis`)

### Takeaway
**NOT NOVEL.** The algorithm is plain Berlekamp–Massey (Massey 1969) on a gap-free clean run. The "adaptive window" is just about 2L of the most recent bits, which Massey's 2L bound already implies. "Drop after two contradictions" is a verify-and-refit heuristic. The code does not tolerate erasures or insertions inside the BM computation: any missed slot resets the run. Prior work on noisy LFSR recovery (correlation attacks, k-error linear complexity, blind scrambler/PN reconstruction) addresses a harder problem than this expert solves. Applying BM to hopping sequences is itself established, both as a known threat to low-linear-complexity FH sequences and as a 2025 Korean ICOIN paper, and the author's own review cites Strickland 2023 and Kim et al. 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Massey, "Shift-register synthesis and BCH decoding" (IEEE Trans. IT 15(1), 1969). BM finds the shortest LFSR for a sequence, and a sequence of linear complexity C is determined by 2C consecutive terms — [Massey 1969 PDF (Stanford)](https://cs359.stanford.edu/massey.pdf); [Wikipedia: Berlekamp–Massey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlekamp%E2%80%93Massey_algorithm)
- Stamp & Martin, "An algorithm for the k-error linear complexity of binary sequences with period 2^n", IEEE Trans. IT 39(4):1398–1401, 1993. This gives the smallest linear complexity reachable by changing at most k terms, i.e. LFSR synthesis that tolerates errors — [ISC record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/969292.htm); [arXiv 1109.4459 (related survey/extension)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.4459.pdf)
- Cluzeau, "Reconstruction of a linear scrambler" (2004/2007). It reconstructs an LFSR-based scrambler from a long output segment under several input assumptions. He Wenjia (IET Communications) reports a hypothesis-testing variant with lower complexity than Cluzeau's — [ISC record (Cluzeau)](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/2055295.htm); [ISC record (He)](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/55743.htm)
- Cluzeau & Finiasz, "Recovering a code's length and synchronization from a noisy intercepted bitstream", ISIT 2009. This is blind reconstruction from a noisy intercept, for linear block codes rather than LFSRs — [ISC record](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/2988159.htm)
- FH-specific: a Chinese-language paper on optimal FH sequence sets (ISCAS) says that for FH sequences with small linear complexity, an eavesdropper can obtain all hopping patterns from short samples using Berlekamp–Massey. A 2025 ICOIN paper treats the hopping pattern as LFSR output and estimates the generator polynomial from a time–frequency image of the intercepted signal. The ICOIN PDF could not be text-extracted; this summary is from the search engine — [ISCAS OpenIR](https://ir.iscas.ac.cn/handle/311060/14646); [ICOIN 2025 abstract A-1-1](https://d2j16w31g89z0j.cloudfront.net/site/icoin2025/abs/A-1-1.pdf)
- Strickland et al., "Predicting Jamming Systems Frequency Hopping Sequences Using Artificial Neural Networks", SoutheastCon 2023. It reverse-engineers LFSR (m-sequence) hopping jammers from as few as 4 samples, and identifies the sequence from about 25 — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5e556e5a7cf05a0b8eabea0abf7d0d5c/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Hong et al., "High Security Frequency/Time Hopping Sequence Generators" (IWSDA 2007), analyse possible attacks on p^k-ary FH/TH generators built from registers and a combinatorial function — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8a2160f927cc5d308a5c5a68c6951fec/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- FH sequence design papers aim to raise linear span specifically to resist BM attacks (e.g. Arasu et al. 2023), so BM-based hop prediction is the assumed threat model in that literature — [Arasu et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/03a9f68cf2fc5e3a9555222ff1a9103e/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Not re-fetched this session (cited in the author's own review): Siegenthaler 1985 (correlation attack); Meier & Staffelbach, *J. Cryptology* 1(3):159–176, 1989 (fast correlation attacks, which recover the LFSR state from a keystream observed through a binary symmetric channel).

### Inferences
- The expert sits below the noise-tolerant state of the art. Correlation attacks and k-error linear complexity handle bit errors, and the scrambler literature handles noisy intercepts. `AdaptiveBMHypothesis` handles noise only by refusing to run BM over a gap and by discarding a fit after two contradictions.
- "LFSRs up to length 32" is a parameter, not a contribution: BM handles any length given 2L clean bits.
- Bit-expanding M = 2^b channels into a GF(2) stream and running BM is the standard approach when the channel index is formed from consecutive register bits. It does not cover generators that take non-adjacent bits per hop. I have not checked which mapping `channels_to_bits` assumes against real hopper designs.
- Defensible wording: "a Berlekamp–Massey expert (Massey 1969) that fits on the shortest sufficient window of clean consecutive hops and discards a fit refuted by two clean observations." Do not claim BM "robust to missed/phantom hops". The robustness comes from waiting for a clean run and from the bank's likelihood weighting.

### Gaps
- I found no paper on LFSR synthesis (unknown feedback polynomial) from sequences with erasures at known positions plus insertions at unknown positions. A sparse search turned up only RS errors-and-erasures decoding (Forney-modified BM) and k-error linear complexity, both adjacent. If OTS later implements BM with erasures or insertions, that could be the most defensible novelty in this slice. It would need its own literature search, including the RS/BCH errors-and-erasures decoding literature and "blind PN sequence estimation" in DSSS.
- Kim et al. 2025 (IEEE TAES, spreading-code estimation using BM, cited by the author) was not re-verified here.

## Q3. Pruned full-keyspace seed search for weak-seed PRNG and LCG (`SeedBeamHypothesis`)

### Takeaway
**NOT NOVEL as an attack.** Exhaustive key/seed search with candidate elimination by observations is the most basic cryptanalytic method. The closest FH-specific prior art is passive Bluetooth Classic clock recovery. There, a sniffer that knows LAP/UAP finds the 27-bit master clock by matching observed hop channels against the hop sequences of candidate clocks until one is confirmed (Ubertooth/libbtbb, Spill & Bittau 2007 onward). That is a pruned search over a hop-generator "seed" (the clock) from partial, gappy channel observations. What may be new is the scoring: a log-likelihood beam with soft P_d/P_fa weighting and a 25-nat pruning margin instead of hard elimination. That is a direct application of standard Bayesian filtering over a finite hypothesis set.

### Cited Findings
- A passive observer who knows a Bluetooth master's LAP and UAP can compute the hop sequence. One packet does not reveal the clock, so the clock is found by matching observed hops against predicted sequences until a guess is confirmed. Spill & Bittau's BlueSniff (WOOT 2007) got the clock by paging the master; later approaches avoid this and are passive. This finding comes from the search-engine summary of arXiv:2002.05126; the PDF could not be text-extracted this session — [arXiv 2002.05126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05126)
- The ubertooth-rx manual says that after the LAP and UAP are recovered, the tool tries to recover the clock value and follow the piconet — [ubertooth-rx man page](https://www.mankier.com/1/ubertooth-rx)
- UAP recovery in Ubertooth tries each of the 64 possible values of the lower six clock bits to narrow the UAP candidates, which is again candidate enumeration against observations — [Great Scott Gadgets, "Discovering the Bluetooth UAP" (2014)](https://greatscottgadgets.com/2014/06-10-discovering-the-bluetooth-uap/)
- The Bluetooth hop sequence depends on the master's LAP, UAP and CLKN. CLK27 is a 27-bit counter at 3200 Hz that wraps in just under 24 hours (D. Spill, 2013) — [Great Scott Gadgets, "Motivating the problem"](https://greatscottgadgets.com/2013/02-07-motivating-the-problem)
- Albazrqaoe, Huang & Xing, "Practical Bluetooth Traffic Sniffing: Systems and Privacy Implications" (BlueEar), MobiSys 2016, pp. 333–345, DOI 10.1145/2906388.2906403. Two coordinated radios plus statistical/ML models learn a target's adaptive hopping behaviour, with reported capture above 90%. Journal version: IEEE/ACM ToN 27(1):71–84, 2019 — [CityU PDF](https://www.cs.cityu.edu.hk/~jhuan9/papers/blueear16mobisys.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/albazrqa/BluEar)
- Cominelli, Gringoli, Patras, Lind & Noubir, "Even Black Cats Cannot Stay Hidden in the Dark: Full-band De-anonymization of Bluetooth Classic Devices", IEEE S&P 2020, DOI 10.1109/SP40000.2020.00091. A full-band SDR sniffer de-anonymises BT Classic devices within seconds (method details not verified) — [CPS-VO record](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/72569); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9152700)
- The Ubertooth to-do list says the clock-trial code assumes AFH is enabled with all channels in use, so channel-map-aware candidate testing is listed as future work — [Ubertooth docs, To do](https://ubertooth.readthedocs.io/en/latest/to_do.html)

### Inferences
- `SeedBeamHypothesis` resembles the Bluetooth clock search: a finite keyspace (2^16–2^18 here, 2^27 clock values in BT), candidates scored by observed channel at known slot times, so missed packets are known-position erasures that cost nothing, and convergence in about log_M(keyspace) clean hops. That bound is the standard information-theoretic count, not a new result.
- The soft-likelihood beam with a nat-margin prune tolerates a phantom hop, whereas hard elimination would kill the true seed. That is a modest, defensible engineering point. It is a standard pattern: particle filtering or beam search over discrete hypotheses with an emission model.
- Defensible wording: "exhaustive weak-seed search, as in Bluetooth clock recovery, run as a pruned log-likelihood beam under the P_d/P_fa sensor model, so that a false-alarm hop costs the true seed a bounded penalty instead of eliminating it."

### Gaps
- I could not open libbtbb's source or the arXiv 2002.05126 PDF to confirm whether Bluetooth clock recovery uses hard elimination (survivor lists) or a soft or vote-based score, and how it handles a mis-detected channel. The "hard elimination" contrast above is my unverified inference.
- I found no military/TRANSEC FH seed-recovery papers in open literature. Such work is likely classified or non-English; absence of evidence is weak.

## Q4. Bayesian program search over ~10k–140k vectorised rule families with Occam prior, pruning, restart-with-carry, ghost-aware re-sync (`RuleSearchHypothesis`)

### Takeaway
**The method is not novel. The application may be novel, with wording that names the application.** Bayesian model averaging over an enumerated class of deterministic programs, with a prior that favours simpler program classes, is standard. Its lineage runs through Solomonoff induction and MDL, Bayesian program learning (Lake et al. 2015; Ellis et al. 2016), and integer-sequence/OEIS program synthesis (Gauthier & Urban, AAAI 2023; d'Ascoli et al., ICML 2022). Restart-on-switch with carried survivors is a heuristic form of switching-experts / change-point mixtures (fixed-share, BOCPD). Two features are more specific: (i) scoring every candidate program through a P_d/P_fa passive-sensor likelihood, with gaps filled by each rule's own prediction, and (ii) applying this to zero-shot FH hop-rule identification. I found no prior work combining these.

### Cited Findings
- Gauthier & Urban, "Learning Program Synthesis for Integer Sequences from Scratch", AAAI 2023, vol. 37(6), pp. 7670–7677 (arXiv:2202.11908). A learned-policy tree search over programs built from basic operators found programs for 27,987 OEIS sequences without human training examples — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/25930); [arXiv 2202.11908](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11908v3.pdf)
- Gauthier, Olšák & Urban, "Alien Coding" (arXiv:2301.11479, 2023). Neural-translation-based self-learning found programs for more than 78,000 OEIS sequences — [arXiv 2301.11479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11479)
- d'Ascoli, Kamienny, Lample & Charton, "Deep Symbolic Regression for Recurrent Sequences" (ICML 2022 title: "...for Recurrence Prediction"), PMLR 162:4520–4536. Transformers infer the recurrence behind integer/float sequences and beat Mathematica's built-in recurrence finding on an OEIS subset — [arXiv 2201.04600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04600); [Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/deep-symbolic-regression-for-recurrent-sequences/)
- Ellis & Solar-Lezama et al., "Sampling for Bayesian Program Learning" (NeurIPS 2016). A description-length prior over programs is used for Bayesian program learning — [NeurIPS 2016 text](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/NeurIPS/2016/sampling_for_bayesian_program_learning__31c297e1.txt)
- Vitányi & Li, "Minimum Description Length Induction, Bayesianism, and Kolmogorov Complexity". Ideal MDL coincides with Bayesian prediction under the universal (Solomonoff) prior under stated conditions, so Occam priors over programs are the textbook Bayes/MDL formulation — [arXiv cs/9901014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/9901014)
- Bayesian ILP with minimum-message-length priors learns programs from **noisy** data (2025) — [arXiv 2508.06230](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06230)
- Not re-fetched this session; standard references from memory, confirm before citing:
  - Lake, Salakhutdinov & Tenenbaum, "Human-level concept learning through probabilistic program induction", *Science* 350:1332–1338, 2015 (DOI 10.1126/science.aab3050).
  - Solomonoff, "A formal theory of inductive inference", *Information and Control*, 1964.
  - Herbster & Warmuth, "Tracking the best expert", *Machine Learning* 32, 1998 (fixed-share switching).
  - Adams & MacKay, "Bayesian online changepoint detection", arXiv:0710.3742, 2007.

### Inferences
- Equal mass per family, split uniformly within the family, is a two-level uniform prior. It is an Occam prior only in the coarse sense that a large family pays log K per rule. It is a standard hierarchical-prior choice, not a new inductive principle.
- The "information-theoretic minimum" lock counts in the docstring (3 hops for an arithmetic progression, d+3 for degree d, P+1 for period P) are properties of any exact Bayes mixture over that library. They should be presented as such, not as a new bound.
- Vectorised evaluation of about 10^5 rules in parallel is an implementation contribution. Enumerative program search is the baseline that the OEIS papers improve on with learned guidance.
- Restart-with-carry (survivors keep half the mass, the fresh library gets the rest) approximates a two-hypothesis change-point mixture (Adams & MacKay) or fixed-share (Herbster & Warmuth). Cite these and call it a heuristic approximation.
- Ghost-aware re-sync (re-sync after two disagreeing clean hops when P_fa > 0) is a sensible domain heuristic. I found no prior work on program-induction sequence predictors that model false-alarm insertions explicitly. The program-synthesis papers above assume exact sequences. The noisy-data work (MML-ILP) handles label noise, not an insertion/erasure channel.
- Defensible wording: "an enumerative Bayesian program-averaging expert over a fixed library of about 10^4–10^5 integer-recurrence rules, in the tradition of Solomonoff/MDL and Bayesian program learning, adapted to a passive FH sensor: candidates are scored through a P_d/P_fa emission model, gaps are bridged by each rule's own predictions, and a change-point-style restart handles rule switches. To our knowledge this is the first application of program-induction sequence prediction to FH hop-rule identification under missed and phantom detections." Keep the "to our knowledge" and the application qualifier.

### Gaps
- I did not search the Chinese-language FH-prediction literature for rule/program-search hop predictors; the author's review lists mostly neural ones. A Chinese-language search for 跳频序列 预测 规则 (hop-sequence prediction rules) and 符号回归 (symbolic regression) is recommended before claiming application novelty.
- I did not verify the statistics/ML literature on "HMM with deterministic hidden program plus insertion/deletion emissions". Profile-HMM-style insertion/deletion channels (bioinformatics) are a conceptual precedent for modelling phantom and missed hops, and are worth a sentence.

## Q5. Recovering the secret/seed from a lossy intercept (FH sequence cracking with erasures and insertions), and the combined expert bank

### Takeaway
**NOVEL-IN-COMBINATION-OR-APPLICATION at most, and only with careful wording.** Every individual mechanism is established. Seed and parameter recovery of LCG and LFSR generators is classical cryptanalysis. Erasure tolerance at known positions is implicit in any time-indexed candidate test (Bluetooth clock recovery). Noisy-keystream LFSR recovery is the correlation-attack literature. Bayesian mixtures of experts and switching mixtures are standard. I found no publication that puts algebraic/brute-force cryptanalytic experts (LCG, BM/LFSR, weak-seed), an enumerative program-induction expert and statistical predictors into one Bayesian hypothesis bank, scored under an explicit P_d/P_fa passive-sensor likelihood, for zero-shot FH next-hop prediction. That gap matches the author's own review (§II-B: published FH predictors assume clean, consecutive outputs and are fit per emitter). This rests on a bounded search, about 15 queries, English only.

### Cited Findings
- Published FH predictors are mostly neural (LSTM, CNN-GRU, TCN) fit on intercepted data, e.g. Li et al. 2021 (LSTM, *China Communications*) and Zhu et al. 2023 (CNN-GRU, *Electronics*) — [Li et al. 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8d2c8a037d7e5b3dbf44d13e0d0185c7/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Zhu et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c8aa0982ffb75491ab207a65ed3d4744/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- Algebraic FH attacks in the open literature are BM against low-linear-complexity sequences (ISCAS paper; ICOIN 2025) and ANN reverse-engineering of LFSR hoppers (Strickland 2023) — [ISCAS OpenIR](https://ir.iscas.ac.cn/handle/311060/14646); [ICOIN 2025](https://d2j16w31g89z0j.cloudfront.net/site/icoin2025/abs/A-1-1.pdf); [Strickland 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5e556e5a7cf05a0b8eabea0abf7d0d5c/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- The closest operational system that recovers a hopping secret from partial passive observation is Bluetooth sniffing (Ubertooth clock recovery; BlueEar; Cominelli et al. S&P 2020). There the generator is public, the secret is address plus clock, and packets are missed — [arXiv 2002.05126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05126); [BlueEar](https://www.cs.cityu.edu.hk/~jhuan9/papers/blueear16mobisys.pdf); [Cominelli et al.](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/72569)
- FH sequence-design papers treat BM and impossible-differential attacks as the threat model and raise linear span or use block ciphers (GOST/DES-based FH generation) to defeat them. This supports the "predictability boundary" framing: keyed cryptographic generators defeat all of these experts — [Chen et al. 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/3ea462fb4f705fddb325fb09e16b019b/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Arasu et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/03a9f68cf2fc5e3a9555222ff1a9103e/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- No paper found applies Berlekamp–Massey to LFSR output corrupted by insertions or deletions; such errors break the alignment BM relies on — [search summary; Massey 1969](https://cs359.stanford.edu/massey.pdf)

### Inferences
- The code does not perform cryptanalysis with insertions. Erasures are handled either by waiting for a clean contiguous run (LCG, BM) or by known-index likelihood scoring (seed beam, rule search). Phantom hops are handled by soft likelihood plus pruning margins, and by two-strike re-sync and re-anchoring. A claim of "secret recovery from a lossy intercept" is accurate for seed beam and rule search. It overstates the case for the LCG and BM experts, which need a gap-free clean run to fit.
- Recommended claim, with "to our knowledge": "We integrate classical generator-recovery attacks (algebraic LCG recovery, Berlekamp–Massey, exhaustive weak-seed search) and an enumerative Bayesian program-induction expert as hypotheses in a single Bayesian bank whose likelihoods use an explicit passive-sensor model (P_d, P_fa). Each attack then contributes only when its assumptions are met, and the bank tolerates missed and phantom hops. The individual attacks are classical; the contribution is their sensor-aware integration for zero-shot FH next-hop prediction."
- Claims to avoid: "novel LCG cryptanalysis", "BM robust to erasures/insertions", "first to crack FH sequences from lossy intercepts" (Bluetooth sniffers already recover hop secrets from partial captures), and "information-theoretically optimal" without stating "relative to the enumerated library".
