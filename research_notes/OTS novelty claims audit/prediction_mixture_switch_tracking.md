# Novelty audit: next-channel prediction, Fixed-Share mixture, regime monitor and SwitchTracker (OTS / fhew)

Scope note: this slice covers four candidate contributions in `fhew/predict.py`, `fhew/mixture.py`, `fhew/regime.py`, `fhew/tracker.py` and `RecurrenceHypothesis` / `ContextMixingHypothesis` in `fhew/experts.py`. What the code actually does (read in this session, nothing modified):

- `ChannelPredictor` (predict.py) wraps a `FixedShareMixture` over a bank of experts that always includes a training-free "universal" expert. Headline metric: top-1 next-channel accuracy plus lift over chance (1/M).
- `FixedShareMixture` (mixture.py): Herbster–Warmuth update `w <- w*exp(-eta*loss)`, then `(1-alpha)w + alpha/N`; loss is one-step log-loss, scored only on slots with exactly one detection (the "clean surrogate"). The whole Bayesian `HypothesisBank` enters as one expert. `predictability()` returns kappa = 1 - H(p_mix)/log M.
- `ContextMixingHypothesis` (experts.py) is a PPM/context-mixing-style variable-order Markov mixture (orders 0..max_order, exponential weights on recent log-loss) — not CTW proper and not Lempel–Ziv.
- `RegimeController` (regime.py) runs CUSUM, a compact Gaussian BOCPD, or a conformal betting e-detector on the mixture's loss stream; on alarm it resets mixture weights and calls `forget(retain)` to flush the context model's tables. Reports recovery time (hops until top-1 returns).
- `SwitchTracker` (tracker.py): Adams–MacKay BOCPD whose run-length hypotheses each carry an anchored copy of the identification bank plus universal experts started at the hypothesised switch slot; switch declared when >= 90 % run-length mass is on young segments; family posterior marginalised over segments; prediction = run-length-weighted mixture; pruning with protected newborn segments; `merge=20` re-dates a takeover within 20 hops instead of counting a second switch.
- `RecurrenceHypothesis`: enumerates every rule `c_t = a1 c_{t-1} + a2 c_{t-2} + b (mod M)` (all of them for M <= 32, |a| <= 8 otherwise), each with its own belief of the last two channels; a clean slot re-syncs, other slots roll forward on the rule's own prediction; rules are scored through the sensor-aware (P_d, P_fa) emission and weighted Bayesianly.
- Author's own measured results (docs/CHANGELOG.md, docs/methodology_and_results.md; not independently verified here): out-of-bank top-1 for `lagged_fib` 0.88 and `quadratic_cg` 0.95 (M=16, P_d=0.95, P_fa=0); CSPRNG top-1 ~0.06 ≈ 1/16; exp18 switch detection 90–93 % across four sensor settings, median delay 1–10 hops.

## Q1. Prior work on predicting FH channels / hopping patterns (accuracy, sensor assumptions, out-of-distribution behaviour)

### Takeaway
Next-hop / FH-pattern prediction is a well-populated topic (LSTM, CNN-GRU, CNN-LSTM, TCN, attention-CNN, HMM, and a neural LFSR-sequence predictor), so "predict the next channel" is not itself new. In what I found, all prior predictors are trained offline (per emitter, or with transfer learning to new patterns). None scores against an explicit detection/false-alarm sensor model, and none reports a generator-by-generator top-1 vs. chance protocol that includes out-of-library generators and a cryptographic control. Those evaluation choices set OTS apart; the prediction task does not.

### Cited Findings
- Li et al. (WCSP 2020) use an LSTM to predict hopping frequencies "point by point" from historical HF frequency data and extend this to frequency-interval prediction. No accuracy figure appears in the abstract. — [IEEE Xplore 9299717](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9299717); [CPS-VO record](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/80208)
- Zhu et al. (Electronics 2023) recover FH sequences with a ResNet-SE CNN + GRU from time–frequency images. Transfer learning handles different hop sets, and performance is reported as BER close to ideal reception. This is supervised training, not training-free prediction. — [MDPI Electronics 12(3):496](https://mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/3/496)
- Strickland et al. (IEEE SoutheastCon 2023) train a neural network on maximal-length LFSR hopping sequences. It reverse-engineers a hopping jammer, driving jamming interference to 0 with as few as 4 hop samples, and decides in about 25 samples whether the jammer uses a sequence the model was trained for. This is the closest FH-specific prior art for "identify the generator, then predict, and recognise out-of-library". — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5e556e5a7cf05a0b8eabea0abf7d0d5c/)
- Basak et al. (IEEE TCCN 2024), "Spectrum Prediction for Protocol-Aware RF Jamming", train a CNN-LSTM on spectrograms to predict future time–frequency occupancy of drone FHSS for jamming. Unseen hopping rates and patterns are predicted via transfer learning, and they validate on real drone signals. — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ed9be6f3d8a3581abd26eb9da5064b0a/); [RMA page](https://radiolab.rma.ac.be/?p=1942)
- Thiele et al. (IEEE PIMRC 2023) build a supervised CNN that predicts the next 25 ms of FHSS time–frequency occupancy from a 50 ms window, with a mean custom score of about 0.9. — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/78ece68fc5bb53538a01edf63cf61755/)
- Y. Li et al. (CNIOT 2026) use a temporal-attention CNN with a hopping-sequence prior and report a mean label-prediction score of 0.903 on DroneRFa DJI Phantom 3 recordings (0.835 for the baseline). — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9e74e05ab3e9516b88b02df776fcde21/)
- J.-Q. Li et al. (ISAES 2026) predict UAV FH with a TCN and report about 68.7 % lower average error than LSTM/GRU baselines. — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f3a376e6f6fd5dbc867e4630a17d11b1/)
- M. Deng et al. (VTC2025-Fall) predict chaotic (nonlinear) hop frequencies with a homotopy-HMM when prior information is lacking. Results are reported as MAE. — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d3a6c701976f506ba93fae0f21f89a66/)
- Metrics in this literature are BER, MAE, custom overlap scores or "LP score", not top-1 next-channel accuracy against a 1/M chance line (inferred from the abstracts above; full texts not read).

### Inferences
- **Candidate 1, "prediction-first framing / top-1 as primary metric": NOT NOVEL as a framing.** Next-hop prediction for jamming is the explicit goal of Strickland 2023, Basak 2024, Li 2020 and others. Defensible wording: "we evaluate by top-1 next-channel accuracy and lift over chance per generator family, including out-of-library generators and a keyed CSPRNG control". That is a distinctive evaluation protocol, not a new task.
- "Falls to chance on a CSPRNG" is an expected sanity property, not a contribution. Present it as a calibration or humility check ("the predictor does not invent structure"), not as a result.
- Strickland 2023 anticipates the "identify generator family, predict, flag out-of-library" idea for LFSR-only banks. OTS differs in a multi-family bank, a lossy sensor and training-free operation, so cite it as closest prior work.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve accuracy numbers for Li et al. 2020, or locate the China Communications 2021 paper cited as [26] in the author's review. Its title, venue and year are unverified; search found the WCSP 2020 paper and a related "spectrum situation prediction" paper by the same group, but not that title.
- Chinese-language journals (e.g., 电子与信息学报, 系统工程与电子技术) were not searched. Hop-prediction work there may include HMM- or Markov-based online predictors.

## Q2. Expert advice / Fixed-Share / switching experts / universal prediction (CTW, PPM, LZ) for RF channel, spectrum or adversary-sequence prediction, including under noisy or erased observations

### Takeaway
All the algorithmic ingredients are classical: Fixed-Share (1998), switching-strategy and Fixed-Share-over-HMM codes (2010–2013), context-tree switching (2012), LZ-based prediction in wireless mobility management (1999/2002), and universal prediction from noisy past observations (2001). Online expert or bandit learning is common in jamming and anti-jamming, but for choosing actions, not for forecasting a hop sequence. I found no paper that combines generator-specific experts with a training-free universal predictor under Fixed-Share for FH next-hop prediction. Treat it as novel in application and combination only.

### Cited Findings
- Fixed-Share comes from Herbster & Warmuth, "Tracking the Best Expert", *Machine Learning* 32:151–178 (1998), DOI 10.1023/A:1007424614876. It competes with the best sequence of experts and gives tracking-regret bounds. — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/mlj/1998/herbster1998mlj-tracking); [author PDF](https://mwarmuth.bitbucket.io/pubs/J52.pdf)
- Koolen & de Rooij, "Universal Codes From Switching Strategies", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 59(11):7168–7185 (2013), combine sequential prediction strategies with switching models expressed as HMMs. Variants cover clustered switches and switches between related strategies. — [arXiv 1311.6536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6536)
- "Switching between Hidden Markov Models using Fixed Share" (arXiv 1008.4532) applies Fixed-Share over HMM predictors. Authorship was not confirmed in this session; I believe it is Koolen & de Rooij, 2010. — [arXiv 1008.4532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.4532)
- Veness, Ng, Hutter & Bowling, "Context Tree Switching", DCC 2012, pp. 327–336, swap CTW's recursive weighting for switching. — [arXiv 1111.3182](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3182); [Bowling pubs page](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~bowling/publications/b2hd-12dcc-cts.html)
- Weissman & Merhav, "Universal Prediction of Individual Binary Sequences in the Presence of Noise", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 47(6):2151–2173 (2001), compete with a set of experts when only noisy observations of the past are available (XOR or additive noise). This is the theoretical ancestor of "expert mixture on a lossy observation stream". — [Technion PDF (Merhav)](https://www.ee.technion.ac.il/people/merhav/papers/predav.pdf); [catalogue record](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1523306.htm)
- Bhattacharya & Das, LeZi-Update (MobiCom 1999; *Wireless Networks* 8:121–135, 2002), use the LZ78 universal compressor as a predictor of user mobility in PCS networks. This is long-standing prior use of LZ universal prediction in wireless systems. — [record](https://annex66.org/?q=node/67)
- Lempel–Ziv complexity has been used to characterise primary-user spectrum activity and to predict when learning helps dynamic channel selection, tested on GSM, ISM and DECT occupancy data. — [TCD record "Complexity of Spectrum Activity and Benefits of Learning for Dynamic Channel Selection"](https://edepositireland.ie/handle/2262/68107)
- Vanerio & Larroca (IEEE TCCN 2020) use online expert-based no-regret algorithms for cognitive-radio secondary spectrum markets. The setting is decisions, not channel-sequence forecasting. — [library record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2374764758)
- Amuru, Tekin, van der Schaar & Buehrer, "Jamming Bandits", IEEE TWC 15(4), 2016, use online (bandit) learning to choose a jammer's physical-layer parameters. The bandit learns actions; it does not predict hops. — [arXiv 1411.3652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.3652)
- K. Li et al. (IEEE TAES 2024) express an unknown jamming strategy as a weighted combination of prior parameterised jammer models learned online. This is the closest EW analogue to "a bank of known adversary models plus online weights". — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c1aa1cee4023579897944c4e9995736c/)
- P. Zhou et al., "Jamsa" (IEEE TNSE 2020), treat link-level PDRs as "expert advice" in a combinatorial contextual bandit for anti-jamming scheduling. — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0e0045c7ce4453b9a9e7f62aea338f89/)
- Zero-shot or in-context prediction of unseen generators: Tao, Doshi, Kalra, He & Barkeshli, "(How) Can Transformers Predict Pseudo-Random Numbers?", ICML 2025 (arXiv 2502.10390). Transformers predict LCG sequences in context with unseen parameters (a, c) up to m = 2^32 and unseen moduli up to m_test = 2^16. A follow-up (Tao & Barkeshli, arXiv 2510.26792, Oct 2025) extends this to permuted congruential generators. — [arXiv 2502.10390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10390); [ICML 2025 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/44774); [2510.26792 listing](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2510.26792v1_enmode)

### Inferences
- **Candidate 1, mechanism (Fixed-Share over generator experts plus an always-on universal predictor): NOVEL-IN-COMBINATION/APPLICATION.** Every component is anticipated: Fixed-Share (Herbster–Warmuth 1998), switching over HMM or strategy experts (Koolen & de Rooij 2010/2013), context-model switching (Veness 2012), and universal prediction from noisy pasts (Weissman–Merhav 2001). The searches found none of these applied to FH hop prediction. Defensible wording: "To our knowledge, this is the first application of tracking-regret expert aggregation (Fixed-Share) to frequency-hopping next-channel prediction. It combines a bank of generator-specific experts with a training-free variable-order context model, so that prediction degrades gracefully, rather than failing, on generators outside the bank."
- **"Zero-shot" is a risky term.** In ML (e.g., Tao et al. 2025) it means a pretrained model generalising without updates. The fhew universal experts are online, training-free learners (PPM / context mixing; brute-force recurrence enumeration) that adapt in context. More precise and defensible wording: "training-free, online universal predictor". Tao et al. 2025 should be cited as the closest "predict unseen PRNG parameters in context" work. A reviewer may also point to classical PRNG-inference results, e.g. lattice and Boyar-style LCG cracking. I did not verify those in this session; they are covered by another slice.
- Fixed-Share's regret guarantee is stated for log-loss on the true sequence. fhew scores only "clean" slots (exactly one detection), so the theoretical tracking bound does not carry over directly to the lossy stream. Any claim that "the Fixed-Share regret bound applies" should be qualified.

### Gaps
- I found no work applying CTW, PPM or LZ specifically to FH hop sequences or spectrum-occupancy sequences, beyond LZ complexity as a feature. The absence rests on a limited search (English, IEEE/arXiv/Consensus).
- The full text of Vanerio & Larroca was not checked for Fixed-Share variants.

## Q3. Change-point / regime-switch detection for emitters or PRNG switching (BOCPD, switching experts), regime monitor, context flush, kappa

### Takeaway
BOCPD with a per-run-length model bank, i.e. joint online change-point detection, model selection and prediction, is published (Adams & MacKay 2007; Fearnhead & Liu 2007; Knoblauch & Damoulas ICML 2018). So is "detect non-stationarity, then reset the learner's posterior" in EW: a 2026 IEEE TAES paper on frequency-agile radar does exactly this. kappa is normalised predictive entropy. I found no work that detects and re-identifies a mid-engagement switch of an FH emitter's sequence generator from a passive lossy sensor stream. That application, and the counter-anchored segment experts it needs, is the defensible novelty.

### Cited Findings
- Adams & MacKay, "Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection", arXiv:0710.3742 (2007). This is the run-length posterior that SwitchTracker uses. It is cited in tracker.py and was not re-fetched in this session; the arXiv ID is from the code docstring and general knowledge. — [arXiv 0710.3742](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)
- Knoblauch & Damoulas, "Spatio-temporal Bayesian On-line Changepoint Detection with Model Selection", ICML 2018 (PMLR 80:2718–2727), extend BOCPD to on-line model selection over a "model universe" of candidates per segment. The algorithm does prediction, model selection and change-point detection jointly and unifies Adams & MacKay (2007) with Fearnhead & Liu (2007). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/knoblauch18a.html); [arXiv 1805.05383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.05383v2.pdf)
- H. Zhu et al., "Online Antijamming Decision-Making for Frequency-Agile Radar in Nonstationary Environments", IEEE TAES 2026, detect changes on rewards and transitions with thresholds tied to a target false-alarm probability. When a change is signalled, the Bayesian posterior is reset "to discard stale information and accelerate adaptation". They report recovery time and recovery rate, which is the same pattern as fhew's alarm → `forget()` → recovery-time metric. — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8eebbabb6b1156028e356ea4704b9da5/)
- Koolen & de Rooij (2013) and Fixed-Share (1998) already give the "switching experts" alternative to explicit change detection (see Q2). — [arXiv 1311.6536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6536); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/mlj/1998/herbster1998mlj-tracking)
- In the FH literature, the change-point and "switch" work I found concerns hop-timing / frequency-switch transients within one signal (e.g., Yuan et al., Electronics Letters 2014, a linearity detector that locates change points between stable signals for RF fingerprinting) and FH signal detection. I found nothing on switches of the generating algorithm. — [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/708dc8ff0dd356528a5768ba591389cb/)
- Anti-jamming RL papers handle "jamming pattern changes" implicitly through learning or retraining (e.g., Liu et al., IEEE Access 2019, use a sliding-window pattern recogniser before channel selection; Zhao et al., VTC2025-Fall, use continual learning to remember reappearing jamming patterns). There is no explicit generator-switch identification. — [Liu 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ebe8664a36b15195a4885ff87559b89d/); [Zhao 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/e1d18f99761351c7b3e4f29a30db170c/)

### Inferences
- **Candidate 2 (regime monitor plus context flush; CUSUM, BOCPD and e-detector on the log-loss stream): NOT NOVEL as a mechanism.** Change detection on a predictor's loss followed by a model reset is standard. Zhu et al. (TAES 2026) is a direct EW precedent for detection → posterior reset → recovery-time evaluation, and Fixed-Share alone already provides switch recovery. The specific engineering observation, that resetting mixture weights is not enough and the context model's tables must also be flushed, is a useful ablation finding, not a publishable novelty. Defensible wording: "we show empirically that weight resets alone under-recover after a generator switch and that flushing the universal predictor's context is required".
- **Candidate 2, kappa = 1 − H(p)/log M: NOT NOVEL.** It is normalised Shannon entropy (efficiency) of the predictive distribution, a textbook quantity, so no paper needs to be cited as anticipating it. Its use to size the number of jamming tones (confidence-adaptive) belongs to the allocation slice. Defensible wording: "a normalised-entropy predictability score", without novelty language. A "confidence-adaptive mixture" claim is only as novel as the downstream use of kappa.
- **Candidate 3 (SwitchTracker): NOVEL-IN-APPLICATION, with an architecture anticipated by Knoblauch & Damoulas 2018 and Adams & MacKay 2007.** In BOCPD, each run-length hypothesis carrying a fresh model instance is the definition of the method. Each hypothesis carrying a whole bank of candidate models with model selection is Knoblauch & Damoulas 2018. Marginalising the family posterior over segments is ordinary Bayesian model averaging. The pieces that are specific to this domain and plausibly new:
  - (a) Segment experts anchored at the hypothesised switch slot, so that counter-based or freshly seeded PRNGs (weak-seed, truncated LCG) restart at counter 0. This is a domain-specific instantiation of the BOCPD restart, but it is what makes re-identification of counter-based hoppers work.
  - (b) Run-length posterior used simultaneously as switch detector, switch dater, generator identifier and next-hop predictor for an FH emitter.
  - (c) The `merge` window, a heuristic for re-dating rather than double-counting switches. It is engineering, not a contribution.

  Defensible wording: "To our knowledge, we are the first to detect, date and re-identify mid-engagement changes of a frequency-hopping emitter's sequence generator from a passive, lossy detection stream. We do this with Bayesian online change-point detection (Adams & MacKay 2007) whose segments carry generator-hypothesis banks (cf. Knoblauch & Damoulas 2018)."
- **RecurrenceHypothesis: NOT NOVEL as a learner; minor novelty in its noise handling.** It is exhaustive Bayesian enumeration of affine order-2 recurrences mod M. Inferring linear and congruential recurrences from outputs is classical (Berlekamp–Massey and LCG inference; see the author's review refs [19]–[24]), and in-context learning of unseen LCG parameters is shown by Tao et al. 2025. The features of the implementation are re-sync on clean single-detection slots, rolling each rule's own prediction across missed hops, and scoring through the P_d/P_fa emission. They amount to a deterministic-transition HMM / particle-style filter per rule, which is sound but standard filtering. Defensible wording: "an erasure- and ghost-tolerant enumerative learner for low-order modular recurrences". Avoid "zero-shot learner" without the qualifier "training-free".

### Gaps
- I did not find any paper on detecting PRNG or algorithm switching (in FH or in generic cryptanalysis). The absence comes from a limited search, and classified or industry EW work would not appear.
- Fearnhead & Liu (2007, JRSS-B) was referenced only through the Knoblauch & Damoulas summary and was not fetched directly.
- No published baseline gives switch-detection delay or dating accuracy for FH emitters, so the exp18 numbers (90–93 % detected, median delay 1–10 hops) cannot be compared to prior art. This also means there is no anticipating result.

## Q4. Operating on a lossy sensor stream (sensor-aware scoring of every expert) and zero-shot prediction of unseen generators

### Takeaway
Blind FH parameter estimation under missing observations exists, as does universal prediction from noisy pasts (theory, binary). The FH predictors I found assume a clean hop sequence or a spectrogram, and none states an explicit P_d/P_fa (missed-hop / ghost-hop) emission model inside a predictor's likelihood. Sensor-aware scoring of every expert, plus prediction carried across missed hops, is therefore novel in application. The theoretical idea, competing with experts from noisy observations, is anticipated by Weissman & Merhav (2001).

### Cited Findings
- Weissman & Merhav (2001) and Weissman, Merhav & Somekh-Baruch (IEEE TIT 47(5):1849–1866, 2001) give universal prediction and finite-state predictability for sequences observed through a noisy channel such as a BSC. — [Technion PDF](https://www.ee.technion.ac.il/people/merhav/papers/predav.pdf); [BIU record (twofold schemes)](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/twofold-universal-prediction-schemes-for-achieving-the-finite-sta-4/)
- The author's own review already cites blind FH estimation with missing observations (atomic-norm soft thresholding, China Commun. 2022) and partial-band observation (IWSSIP 2025). These are parameter estimation, not sequence prediction. — source: author's attached review, refs [35]–[36] (not re-verified here)
- Supervised FH predictors (Zhu 2023, Basak 2024, Thiele 2023, Y. Li 2026) take spectrograms or time–frequency images as input. They handle noise implicitly through training, with no explicit detection-probability model. — [Zhu 2023](https://mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/3/496); [Basak 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ed9be6f3d8a3581abd26eb9da5064b0a/); [Thiele 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/78ece68fc5bb53538a01edf63cf61755/)
- For unseen generators, in-context transformer prediction of LCG/PCG sequences with unseen parameters is established (Tao et al. ICML 2025; Tao & Barkeshli 2025), on clean outputs only. — [arXiv 2502.10390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10390)
- Basak et al. 2024 reach unseen hopping patterns only via transfer learning (retraining), and Zhu et al. 2023 likewise use transfer learning across hop sets. — [Basak 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ed9be6f3d8a3581abd26eb9da5064b0a/); [Zhu 2023](https://mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/3/496)

### Inferences
- **Candidate 4 (sensor-aware scoring of each expert on a lossy (P_d, P_fa) stream): NOVEL-IN-APPLICATION.** Defensible wording: "every expert, including the mixture and the switch tracker, is scored by an explicit detection-probability / false-alarm emission likelihood. Predictions therefore remain usable when hops are missed or ghost detections occur. Prior FH predictors assume clean hop sequences or learn noise implicitly from training spectrograms." Cite Weissman & Merhav 2001 as the theoretical antecedent.
- Two caveats on this claim:
  - The P_d/P_fa model is a simple memoryless Bernoulli detection / uniform-ghost model. A reviewer will note that real interception errors are correlated (fading, collisions, interleaved emitters), so the claim should be scoped to "under an i.i.d. detection/false-alarm sensor model".
  - The Fixed-Share update uses only clean single-detection slots, so the mixture's weights are not sensor-aware in the full likelihood sense, even though members' internal states are. Wording should not overstate this.
- **Unseen generators:** the defensible claim is empirical, not categorical: "without any training, the predictor attains top-1 0.88 / 0.95 on two generators no expert was built for (lagged-Fibonacci, quadratic congruential) under the stated sensor, and is at chance on a keyed CSPRNG". These are the author's numbers, at M=16 with a near-perfect sensor (P_d=0.95, P_fa=0). Tao et al. 2025 should be acknowledged as showing that learned models can predict unseen congruential generators in context. The fhew contribution is doing so without training and under detection losses.

### Gaps
- No prior work was found that reports FH prediction accuracy as a function of P_d / P_fa, so there is no external benchmark for the lossy-sensor results.
- The out-of-bank results reported in the docs are at P_d=0.95, P_fa=0. Whether the out-of-bank advantage holds at "typical" (0.8, 0.02) or "contested" (0.6, 0.05) sensors was not checked in the docs read here. Novelty claims about "lossy" operation for out-of-bank generators should cite the specific sensor settings tested.
- Verdict summary (for the report writer):
  - C1 framing: NOT NOVEL.
  - C1 mechanism (Fixed-Share + universal expert for FH): NOVEL-IN-COMBINATION/APPLICATION.
  - C2 kappa: NOT NOVEL.
  - C2 regime monitor + flush: NOT NOVEL (Zhu et al. TAES 2026; Fixed-Share).
  - C3 SwitchTracker: NOVEL-IN-APPLICATION (architecture anticipated by Adams & MacKay 2007 and Knoblauch & Damoulas 2018).
  - C3 RecurrenceHypothesis: NOT NOVEL as a learner; minor noise-handling novelty.
  - C4 sensor-aware scoring: NOVEL-IN-APPLICATION (theory anticipated by Weissman & Merhav 2001).
  - All "first" claims rest on English-language IEEE/arXiv/Consensus searches (about 15 queries) and should carry "to our knowledge".
