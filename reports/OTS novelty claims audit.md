# Integration, not algorithms, carries OTS's novelty

Most of what the OTS/`fhew` codebase contributes is new as a combination or as an application, not as a new algorithm. Every core method has a clear published ancestor: the detection-level likelihood, MSPRT and e-process stopping, Fixed-Share, Bayesian online change-point detection, context-mixing prediction, LCG and Berlekamp–Massey recovery, bounded seed search, enumerative program induction, and knapsack-style resource allocation. The strongest claim the author can safely make, and also the best evidenced, is narrow: to our knowledge, OTS is the first passive frequency-hopping (FH) analysis system that scores a heterogeneous bank of generator hypotheses, including an explicit none-of-the-above class, through one shared detection-level likelihood, and uses it to identify the generator, forecast the next hop without training, and detect and re-identify generator switches mid-engagement. The attached October 2026 draft locates its novelty elsewhere: in a Mamba-2 encoder, an online conformal set layer, a distribution-free bound on jamming effect forgone, and a PSRO exploitability certificate. **None of those four exists in the code.** The one flagship piece that does exist, the Conformal Decision Theory (CDT) controller, has its stated guarantee undercut by the author's own experiment configuration. A single uncited 2025 ICML paper on transformers predicting pseudo-random numbers could pre-empt the predictor's "zero-shot" story, and Bluetooth hop-sniffing research is the obvious precedent for the whole intercept-predict-respond loop, also uncited. The paper is publishable, but only if it is re-centred on the identification, prediction and switch-tracking core that the code and experiments actually support, with the resource-allocation coupling presented as a demonstrated downstream use whose flagship status is earned only after two cheap experiments are run.

## The repository implements about half of the drafted paper

The attached related-work section (dated 21 Oct 2026, matching thesis Revision 3.1) frames the contribution as "the coupling of a calibrated hop predictor to a budgeted, threshold-aware multi-tone power allocation under an explicit, adversarially valid risk guarantee." It builds subsections around a structured-state-space encoder ("the backbone our encoder adopts"), SAOCP/DtACI/ACI set calibration, Conformal Risk Control, PSRO exploitability, and a graph-attention emitter sorter with Kalman tracking. A grep across `fhew/`, `experiments/`, `tests/` and `dashboard/` finds **no Mamba/S4 encoder, no conformal prediction-set layer, no CRC, no PSRO or exploitability metric, no learned (DQN/PPO) adversaries, no GNN sorter or Kalman tracker, and no active-probing component**. The simulation is single-emitter; bearing and emitter-ID exist only as schema fields. The thesis's TD-MPC2 expert is a recency-weighted `BehaviourHypothesis` whose own docstring calls it a stand-in.

What is built is the identification bank, a Fixed-Share next-channel predictor with a training-free context-mixing expert, a regime monitor, a BOCPD switch tracker, structured-sequence experts, a CDT scalar controller with a threshold-aware knapsack allocator and a reactive-follower baseline, a feasibility map, and a dashboard. The net implemented system is Layer 2 plus a slice of Layers 3–4 of the thesis stack; the encoder (Layer 1), the conformal-set layer, the sorter (Layer 0), the probe and the game (Section 9) are paper-only.

The code has also moved past the draft. Three later commits add material the draft never mentions: the switch tracker, confidence-capped multi-tone allocation (`5488d8b`) and structured-sequence prediction (`90e2e86`). These partly answer the draft's own II-B "gap" paragraph, which now reads as a description of the author's earlier code rather than of the field. The evidence base has two weaknesses a reviewer will notice. First, `results/` is git-ignored (`.gitignore:12`), and **the only committed result JSON is `exp19_sequence_coverage.json`**; every other headline number lives only in prose and committed PNGs, so all exp1–exp18 figures below are "as recorded by the author", not reproduced in this audit. Second, the 2026-11-03 CHANGELOG entry states the exp14, exp15 and exp18 figures still show the pre-change predictor. The README's "126 tests" is also stale: the suite now collects 154.

## The identification bank applies a standard likelihood to a new kind of bank

The sensor model (`fhew/sensor.py`) is slot-synchronous: the true channel is detected with probability P_d, every other channel fires independently with probability P_fa, and the per-slot emission reduces to a log-ratio with the common all-false-alarm factor cancelling across hypotheses. This is the discrete-channel form of the detection/clutter likelihood behind probabilistic data association ([Bar-Shalom, Daum & Huang 2009](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/12313)). Cognitive-radio spectrum prediction already folds a sensor's false-alarm and missed-detection rates into HMM emissions ([Wang & Adriman, EURASIP JWCN 2014](https://jwcn-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s13638-014-0238-5)), and FH signals have been tracked with particle filters since 2006–2009 ([Valyrakis et al., IEEE TSP 2009](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/stochastic-modeling-and-particle-filtering-algorithms-for-trackin/)). The exhaustive state enumeration in `DeterministicTrajectoryFilter` is mathematically a correlation-attack-style maximum-likelihood state search with the binary symmetric channel replaced by a detection channel; the harder synchronization problem (insertions/deletions) is already addressed by the edit-distance correlation-attack line ([Golić & Mihaljević, Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-49264-X_20.pdf)). What prior work has not done, to our knowledge, is run **one shared P_d/P_fa likelihood across competing generator families** (LFSR, LCG, weak seed, Markov, chaos, and a uniform unknown class). That is the defensible contribution, and exp7 supports it (0.98 vs 0.60 for the naive emission at P_d=0.5). Two scoping caveats hold: the sensor is "matched" (the same object generates and scores data), so robustness to a mis-specified P_d/P_fa is untested; and misses occur at known slot positions, so the system tolerates erasures and spurious detections but not timing insertions/deletions, and it does not estimate hop timing. The code's own term for this, "gap-robust Berlekamp–Massey", over-claims; "state-enumeration recovery under a detection-level channel" is accurate.

The stopping rules are classical. Stopping when the top posterior reaches 1−δ is exactly the MSPRT of [Baum & Veeravalli (1994)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6ed7854bb0b65f7eac9c4459158cb464/?utm_source=claude_desktop), itself motivated by spread-spectrum acquisition; its optimality under correlated (HMM) observations is [Dragalin, Tartakovsky & Veeravalli (1999)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ce76bd6b242f5a58a3ce30b10875c971/?utm_source=claude_desktop). The exp11 law E[τ] ∝ log(1/δ) and ∝ 1/P_d is therefore a sanity check reproducing textbook asymptotics, not a finding. The e-process is a Bayes factor against a prior-weighted mixture of competitors; the code confirms the denominator is `logsumexp(log_rho + cum_loglik[mask])` (`fhew/stopping.py:96`), so Ville's inequality ([Ramdas et al., Statistical Science 2023](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-38/issue-4/Game-Theoretic-Statistics-and-Safe-Anytime-Valid-Inference/10.1214/23-STS894.full)) gives a false-identification bound only under that mixture. Against a single competitor j, the bound is merely β/ρ_j, much weaker than β. No applied RF paper using e-process vocabulary was found, so "first use of anytime-valid e-process stopping for FH generator identification" is defensible but modest, and must carry "under the prior-weighted mixture of in-bank competitors".

The H_0 class is the old no-match idea. [Gutman (1989)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f408449295755e79bf43e768163f3a98/?utm_source=claude_desktop) proved a likelihood-ratio test with a no-match decision asymptotically optimal for Markov sources, and open-set rejection is active in RF fingerprinting ([Karunaratne et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13099v1)). OTS differs in a real but old way: H_0 is a generative hypothesis entering the posterior and the stopping accounting, not a threshold on a discriminative score. Its uniform law is well-motivated for a keyed CSPRNG, which is computationally indistinguishable from uniform, so "routes to unknown" on CSPRNG is justified (87%, hard false-ID 0.00–0.03). It does not detect structured out-of-bank generators: a filtered LFSR falls back to the Markov expert. Claims should say "avoids hard false identification of a deterministic family", not "detects unknown generators". The "pairwise SPRT false-identifies 100%" comparison is a straw man because any closed-set test must name a class; the fair baseline is a reject-option classifier, and reviewers will ask for sensitivity to the 0.15 prior floor.

The feasibility map composes classical facts, but one rule is wrong. `fhew/feasibility.py:62` computes `clean_consecutive = distinct_hops * (rp.p_d ** 2)` with the comment "a gap breaks the run", whereas the probability a run of 2L hops is fully detected is P_d^(2L). At L=7, P_d=0.9 the rule credits 0.81 of hops while P_d^14 ≈ 0.23, so the map overstates where plain BM succeeds. Neither κ nor the map is a "predictability bound" in the Fano-type sense of [Song et al. (Science 2010)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1177170); each is an operational heuristic and should be labelled one.

## Prediction and switch tracking are unoccupied in FH, but "zero-shot" is already taken

Forecasting the next hop is an established task: LSTM prediction ([Li et al., WCSP 2020](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9299717)), CNN-GRU recovery with transfer learning ([Zhu et al., Electronics 2023](https://mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/3/496)), CNN-LSTM spectrogram prediction of drone FHSS validated on real signals ([Basak et al., IEEE TCCN 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ed9be6f3d8a3581abd26eb9da5064b0a/)), and neural reverse-engineering of LFSR hoppers with an out-of-library decision in about 25 samples ([Strickland et al., SoutheastCon 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5e556e5a7cf05a0b8eabea0abf7d0d5c/)). Strickland is the closest FH-specific precedent for "identify, predict, flag unknown", but it is LFSR-only, trained, and clean-data. None of these predictors scores against an explicit P_d/P_fa sensor, and none reports per-generator top-1 against a 1/M chance line with out-of-library generators and a CSPRNG control. That evaluation protocol is distinctive; the task is not new.

The mixture's components are all classical: Fixed-Share ([Herbster & Warmuth 1998](https://mlanthology.org/mlj/1998/herbster1998mlj-tracking)), switching over HMM/strategy experts ([Koolen & de Rooij 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.6536)), context-tree switching ([Veness et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3182)), and universal prediction from noisy pasts ([Weissman & Merhav 2001](https://www.ee.technion.ac.il/people/merhav/papers/predav.pdf)). The application is new to our knowledge: Fixed-Share over generator-specific experts plus an always-on, training-free context-mixing model for FH next-hop prediction. Reported out-of-bank top-1 is 0.88 for lagged-Fibonacci (vs 0.42 identification-only) and 0.95 for quadratic-congruential, with CSPRNG near 1/16, all at M=16, **P_d=0.95, P_fa=0** (a near-perfect sensor). The draft's "pretrained to generalize zero-shot" is the wrong description and the wrong word: the code does no pretraining. A reviewer will hold "zero-shot" against [Tao, Doshi, Kalra, He & Barkeshli, "(How) Can Transformers Predict Pseudo-Random Numbers?" (ICML 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10390), which shows transformers predicting linear-congruential outputs in context for unseen parameters and moduli up to 2^16, with a follow-up extending to permuted congruential generators ([Tao & Barkeshli, arXiv 2510.26792](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2510.26792v1_enmode)). OTS's honest difference is being training-free and online, working under detection losses, and being coupled to an identification bank. The draft should use "training-free online universal predictor" and cite Tao et al. as closest work. One further caveat: Fixed-Share's tracking-regret bound assumes log-loss on the true sequence, but `FixedShareMixture` scores only single-detection slots, so the bound does not carry over to the lossy stream and any "the regret bound applies" wording must be qualified.

The regime monitor is not a new mechanism. Detecting a change on a predictor's loss, resetting the posterior, and reporting recovery time is precisely what [Zhu et al. (IEEE TAES 2026)](https://consensus.app/papers/details/8eebbabb6b1156028e356ea4704b9da5/) do for frequency-agile radar; Fixed-Share alone already recovers from switches. The specific observation that weight resets under-recover and the context tables must also be flushed (1.2–2.3× faster in exp15) is a useful ablation, not a publishable novelty. The SwitchTracker is the stronger claim. A BOCPD run-length posterior whose hypotheses each carry a bank of candidate models is the architecture of [Knoblauch & Damoulas (ICML 2018)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/knoblauch18a.html), built on [Adams & MacKay (2007)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742). The domain-specific parts appear new: segment experts anchored at the hypothesised switch slot so counter-based and freshly seeded generators restart at zero, and one posterior that detects, dates, re-identifies and predicts simultaneously. The FH change-point literature found addresses hop-timing transients, not changes of the generating algorithm. Recorded exp18 results are roughly 90%+ of switches detected, next-hop 55–79% by hops 6–10 after a switch, and 0–3% dangerous locks; these predate the current predictor and have no external baseline. `RecurrenceHypothesis` is exhaustive Bayesian enumeration of order-2 affine recurrences; its erasure/ghost handling is sound filtering, not a new learner.

## The recovery experts are classical, and the allocation flagship is under-evidenced

`LCGCryptanalysisHypothesis` solves the easiest case in a long literature: known modulus equal to M, untruncated output, at least six gap-free samples. [Reeds (1977)](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~cs812-1/Reeds.pdf) already recovered the modulus too, and [Plumstead (FOCS 1982)](https://www.scilit.net/publications/1ee35bbec0e9fd4475892dfead3752e0), Boyar, Stern and Frieze et al. broke truncated generators with unknown parameters, with lattice refinements to 2024 ([ePrint 2022/1134](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1134.pdf)). The truncated arm of `SeedBeamHypothesis` brute-forces 2^16 seeds with known constants, which is trivial; its only defensible wrinkle is soft-likelihood pruning under the sensor model, the same pattern as particle/beam search over discrete hypotheses, and its closest operational analogue is Bluetooth passive clock recovery (Ubertooth/libbtbb). `AdaptiveBMHypothesis` is plain [Massey (1969)](https://cs359.stanford.edu/massey.pdf) on a contiguous clean run that any gap resets; the "adaptive window" is Massey's 2L bound, and noise-tolerant synthesis belongs to k-error linear complexity ([Stamp & Martin 1993](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/969292.htm)) and correlation attacks, which are harder problems the expert does not solve. Its exp19 numbers make the ceiling concrete: a 16-bit LFSR is recovered perfectly on a clean stream but collapses to 0.12 top-1 once the sensor is lossy. `RuleSearchHypothesis` is the one recovery expert with a narrow application claim: enumerative Bayesian program-averaging over a fixed library of ~10^4–10^5 integer-recurrence rules, in the Solomonoff/MDL and Bayesian-program-learning tradition ([Ellis et al. 2016](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/NeurIPS/2016/sampling_for_bayesian_program_learning__31c297e1.txt); [Gauthier & Urban, AAAI 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/25930); [d'Ascoli et al., ICML 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04600)), but here scored through a P_d/P_fa emission with gaps bridged by each rule's own prediction. Those program-synthesis papers assume exact sequences, so "to our knowledge first application of program-induction sequence prediction to FH hop-rule identification under missed and phantom detections" is defensible with the qualifier. The committed `exp19_sequence_coverage.json` is the only independently checkable result in the repo and supports the structured-type recovery figures.

On the allocation side, the threshold-aware greedy knapsack (rank channels by predicted-probability-per-unit-denial-cost, fund to a threshold, cap at `max_tones`) is a clean formulation not found stated for FH; prior combinatorial treatments go straight to DRL ([Rao et al., IEEE Sensors J. 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/b873d077d3cd5c9ba1474ab27dac29a1/?utm_source=claude_desktop)) or water-filling. It is defensible as a formulation ("a novel formulation, not the first multi-channel allocator"). The confidence-to-tone-count map (`tones_from_confidence`, verified in `allocator.py:124` as `n = round(min + (max−min)(1−κ))`) occupies a genuinely unoccupied seam: the nearest predictive-jammer work sizes action by a *fixed* budget with proportional inclusion ([Chen & Zhou 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06514)) or scales power rather than count ([Erpek et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02567)). But the map is a one-line linear rule, so the claim is the *coupling* to a calibrated entropy score, not the formula, and it has **no Monte-Carlo experiment** — only five unit tests and the dashboard (status R). The CDT controller (`CDTController`, `allocator.py:257`) is the drafted flagship and the most exposed. Applying Conformal Decision Theory ([Lekeufack et al. 2023, ICRA 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05921)) to RF allocation is new to our knowledge, and that citation itself checks out. But the controller is ~30 lines plus a passing unit test; no experiment reports realised effect-forgone against the target α over an adaptive sequence. Worse, in exp16 the controller is constructed with `alpha=0.2` while every allocation reward in panel A is far below the implied effect level, so the realised loss sits well above 0.2 and the λ knob saturates — the configuration does not demonstrate the "long-run average loss ≤ α" property it is cited for. The surrounding conformal-set and PSRO scaffolding the draft leans on is unimplemented, so the single most novel drafted claim is the least evidenced in code.

Two external bounds on the system-level claims deserve explicit treatment. Bluetooth/BLE hop-prediction research — BtleJack-style CSA#2 reverse engineering and the MSU practical Bluetooth hopping work ([BlueEar, MobiSys 2016](https://www.cs.cityu.edu.hk/~jhuan9/papers/blueear16mobisys.pdf)) — is a real, deployed instance of intercepting an FH link, inferring the hop sequence from generator structure, and acting on it, years earlier. It means the *concept* of the loop is not new; OTS's framing must be "generator-agnostic, bank-plus-universal, under a lossy passive sensor", and the draft currently cites zero protocol-attack literature. And the "no open FH-EW environment" claim (thesis II-D) is bounded by the released, scored [Turing Synthetic Radar Dataset / deinterleaving challenge (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03856); it survives only if narrowed to FH with a dumb-to-adaptive generator library and a passive sensor, and it sits awkwardly against the `GROUNDING` table, in which every parameter is still `source:"assumed"` (RFUAV is named as a placeholder, nothing is calibrated), contradicting the thesis's "calibrated against public datasets".

## Conclusion: what to claim, what to cut, what to fix, and what to run

**Rank these as the safely claimable contributions**, all under a "to our knowledge" posture. (1) A unified sensor-aware generator-identification bank: one P_d/P_fa detection-level likelihood scoring a heterogeneous family set plus an explicit generative H_0, with MSPRT and e-process stopping (qualified to the in-bank mixture null). This is the best-evidenced novel-in-combination claim (exp7, exp4/5, exp9). (2) The switch tracker: first system to detect, date and re-identify a mid-engagement change of an FH emitter's generator from a passive lossy stream, with switch-anchored segment banks. (3) The training-free, online universal predictor coupled to the bank, degrading gracefully on out-of-bank generators and falling to chance on a keyed CSPRNG — explicitly not "zero-shot". (4) The Bayesian rule-search expert as the first program-induction hop predictor under missed and phantom detections. (5) The threshold-aware knapsack and the confidence-to-tone-count coupling, as formulations and as a demonstrated downstream use of the calibrated prediction — not as the flagship, and not as any "first predictive/multi-tone jammer".

**Over-claims to remove from the draft:** the Mamba-2/S4 encoder ("the backbone our encoder adopts") and all SAOCP/DtACI/ACI, CRC, PSRO/exploitability, GNN-sorter, Kalman and probing-bandit material that describes unbuilt components as contributions; "zero-shot" wherever it means pretraining; "distribution-free bound on jamming effect forgone" as a demonstrated result (the CDT guarantee is not evidenced and is violated in exp16's config); "gap-robust Berlekamp–Massey" (say "state-enumeration recovery"); "predictability bound" for κ or the feasibility map; "detects unknown generators" (say "avoids hard false identification of a known family"); any unqualified "no open FH-EW environment" and "calibrated against public datasets"; and the stale "126 tests" and exp14/15/18 figures.

**Citations to fix or add.** Add and differentiate from [Tao et al. 2025 (ICML)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10390) and the follow-up, the sharpest missing prior work for in-context PRNG prediction. Add the Bluetooth/BLE hop-attack line (BlueEar; BtleJack) as the application precedent. Add the Turing TSRD benchmark where the environment claim is made. Add, for honest positioning, Gutman 1989, Baum & Veeravalli 1994, Dragalin et al. 1999, Ramdas et al. 2023, Weissman & Merhav 2001, Knoblauch & Damoulas 2018, and the Reeds/Plumstead/Stern LCG lineage. **Verify or replace before submission:** [15] Zhang–Wu TIFS 2025 (the closest cooperative/multi-tone prior art — two searches could not confirm it exists as cited, and it is load-bearing); the placeholder-author [48]–[50] (Global PSRO maps to McAleer et al. 2022; the GNN-sorter refs are for an unbuilt layer and could simply be dropped); [24] Kim TAES 2025 and [29] Deng VTC2025-Fall; and the [1]/[7] double-listing, where [1] should point to cognitive-radio/radar foundations (Haykin, Mitola), not a 2025 fast-frequency-hopping paper.

**Cheap code/experiment changes that would most strengthen the weakest claims.** First, add a CDT experiment that actually reports realised effect-forgone against α over an adaptive sequence, with a sensible α matched to the achievable effect range, and switch (or add) the e-process denominator to max_{j≠k} p_j to earn a per-competitor false-ID guarantee from Dragalin et al. — both are small edits that convert the two most-exposed flagship claims from R to E. Second, add a mis-specified-sensor experiment (true P_d/P_fa ≠ assumed) and re-run the out-of-bank prediction at the "typical" (0.8, 0.02) and "contested" (0.6, 0.05) settings, since every out-of-bank number is currently at the near-perfect sensor.
